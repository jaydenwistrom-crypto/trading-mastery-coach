## Confirmed Globex-trap setup context

- Globex refers to the electronic session outside regular trading hours (`04:34–06:38`).
- Before regular hours begin, mark the high and low formed during the Globex/overnight session (`07:38–08:40`).
- Momentum traders may buy above the Globex high or short below the Globex low (`08:46–09:49`).
- A breakout is not automatically a trap. The instructor says it must be qualified by whether price is moving into demand or supply (`09:58–11:06`).
- The setup requires an area of institutional demand or supply outside the Globex range (`10:47–11:06`; class notes pages 8–9).
- A **Globex bear trap** is the long-side context: price moves below the Globex low into institutional demand, especially with an overall uptrend and consistent range (page 10).
- A **Globex bull trap** is the short-side context: price moves above the Globex high into institutional supply, especially with an overall downtrend and consistent range (page 11).
- The names describe the group being trapped. A bull trap traps breakout buyers and is a short context; a bear trap traps breakout sellers and is a long context (`11:16–12:28` for the bull-trap naming clarification).
- The chart walkthrough emphasizes alignment with the macro monthly, weekly, and daily trend (`19:29–25:24` for the bull-trap example; `27:19–34:50` for the bear-trap example).
- `IBI` is defined in the video as **impulse–basing–impulse**. The instructor calls a high-quality IBI supply or demand zone outside the Globex range essential (`35:54–36:31`).
- A slide mentions a Globex range of 75%–150% of daily ATR, but the instructor explicitly says he usually does not use that rule and that it is not required (`35:18–36:07`). It is therefore **not** encoded as a mandatory filter.
