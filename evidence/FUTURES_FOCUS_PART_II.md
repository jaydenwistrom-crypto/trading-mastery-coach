## Confirmed topics

- A contract-specific symbol contains a product root, a month code, and a year. The worked example is `GCQ23`: Gold, August, 2023.
- The front month is the contract with the most trading volume. A back month is the next contract in the product's calendar cycle.
- Rollover occurs when liquidity shifts and back-month volume exceeds front-month volume. At that point, the back month becomes the new front month.
- An adjusted continuous chart removes rollover gaps to produce smoother history. An unadjusted continuous chart preserves the price differences between contract months.
- Continuous charts join front-month contracts at rollover dates and can help show longer-term IOF structure.
- Contract-specific charts show one month/year contract from its first trading date and are described in the notes as more useful for finding trade opportunities.
- A rollover gap is the price difference between two contract months at rollover. A natural gap is an actual gap while the market is closed or not trading continuously.
- Contract specifications include the underlying, contract size, expiration/last trading day, minimum tick and dollar value, trading hours, and cash or physical settlement.
